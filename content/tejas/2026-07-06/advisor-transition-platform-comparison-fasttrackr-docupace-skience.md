Choosing a transition platform comes down to how each system handles repapering, ACATS rejects, and document work under deadline. FastTrackr AI leads with AI document intelligence and NIGO pre-validation built specifically for advisor moves. Docupace brings broad digital operations scale. Skience brings a Salesforce-native data model. The right pick depends on which part of the transition hurts most.

Broker-dealers and RIAs evaluating these three usually start with a feature grid from each vendor's site and end up more confused than when they started, because the vendors describe different things using the same words. This comparison reframes the decision around the parts of a transition that actually break, and the criteria that predict whether a platform will hold up when you move a real book.

## Start with the problem, not the feature list

A transition has a few distinct pain points, and different platforms are built around different ones. Before comparing vendors, name the part of your move that costs you the most time and risk today:

- **Document work.** Statements, account forms, and applications that have to be read, validated, and re-keyed. This is where NIGO rates are born.
- **ACATS and repapering.** Getting transfers through the NSCC's Automated Customer Account Transfer Service without a wave of rejects.
- **Data and CRM.** Where client and account records live during and after the move.
- **Ops orchestration.** Tracking hundreds of accounts through concurrent stages without losing one.

Whichever of these is your bottleneck should drive the choice. A platform that is excellent at CRM data modeling does little for you if your problem is a 20 percent NIGO rate on inbound paperwork.

## The criterion most buyers underweight: NIGO before submission

Most platform demos show you the happy path. The real test is what happens to the account that arrives with a title mismatch or a restricted asset. Rejects cluster into a handful of predictable reason categories, and a small number of them account for the majority of rebooks. The platforms that reduce transition time are the ones that catch those errors before submission, not the ones that track rejects after the fact.

When you evaluate any of the three, ask the vendor to walk through exactly how a flawed account form is caught and corrected before it becomes a NIGO. Our breakdown of [ACATS reject reason codes and how pre-validation eliminates them before submission](https://fasttrackr.ai/blog/acats-reject-reason-codes-eliminate-before-submission) gives you the specific failure modes to test against.

This is where [document intelligence](https://fasttrackr.ai/solutions/document-intelligence) earns its place. Reading a statement and pre-filling a form is only useful if it also validates the fields that cause rejects. Extraction without validation just moves the re-keying earlier.

## The second criterion: does it fit how transitions actually run

A book does not move in one clean batch. It moves as hundreds of accounts at different stages at once, under a compliance clock. The platform has to show you, at a glance, which accounts are stuck and why. Evaluate each system on whether it gives an operations lead a live view of exception handling, not just a queue of tasks.

This is the difference between a general workflow tool and a purpose-built [advisor transition platform](https://fasttrackr.ai/solutions/advisor-transitions). The transition-specific system is organized around the move itself: resignation, repaper, ACATS, confirmation. A general ops tool makes you build that structure yourself.

For teams running many moves at once, the orchestration layer matters even more. ## How to run the actual bake-off

Do not evaluate on feature lists. Evaluate on a real sample. Take 20 to 30 of your messiest historical account forms, the ones that caused rejects last quarter, and run them through each platform's process. Measure three things: how many fields were extracted correctly, how many reject-causing errors were flagged before submission, and how long the whole batch took. That head-to-head on your own dirty data tells you more than any demo.

## FAQ

**Which platform is best for a breakaway advisor going independent?**
It depends on your bottleneck. If your pain is inbound paperwork volume and NIGO risk on repapering, a document-intelligence-first platform like FastTrackr AI targets that directly. If you are a larger firm standardizing many operational workflows, Docupace's broad footprint may fit better. If you are committed to Salesforce as your system of record, Skience's native data model is worth weighing.

**Do these platforms replace an attorney during a breakaway?**
No. Software handles document processing, validation, ACATS tracking, and workflow orchestration. Broker Protocol interpretation, employment agreement review, and litigation risk require a qualified attorney. The right split is to automate the repetitive operational work and reserve legal judgment for counsel.

**What is the single most important evaluation criterion?**
How the platform prevents NIGO before submission. Rejects are the main driver of transition delay, and they cluster into a few predictable categories. A platform that validates account data before it hits ACATS saves more time than one that only reports rejects after they happen.

**How should I test these platforms before buying?**
Run a real sample of your worst historical account forms through each one. Measure extraction accuracy, how many reject-causing errors get flagged pre-submission, and total processing time. A bake-off on your own dirty data beats any scripted demo because it shows how each system handles the exceptions that actually slow you down.
