Pick your advisor transition platform by the move you are actually running, not by a feature checklist. A solo breakaway advisor, a recruiting team running many transitions at once, and a consultant repapering for many clients each need a different tool. FastTrackr AI fits transition-speed and NIGO reduction, Docupace fits enterprise document workflow, and Skience fits Salesforce-native firms.

## The criteria that actually predict fit

Before the scenarios, understand what separates these platforms at an architectural level, because the differences are structural, not cosmetic.

**Workflow scope.** Some tools own one stage of the transition. Others try to run the whole repaper from data intake to funded account. Docupace is a document management and workflow backbone that firms typically wire into a broader stack. Skience is built natively on Salesforce, so it inherits Salesforce's CRM strength and its implementation weight. FastTrackr's [advisor transition platform](https://fasttrackr.ai/solutions/advisor-transitions) is purpose-built to cover the end-to-end repaper, with pre-submission validation as the core mechanic rather than an add-on.

**Where the NIGO reduction comes from.** Not-in-good-order rejects are the tax on every transition. A document management system reduces NIGO by standardizing storage and routing. A transition-specific tool reduces it earlier, by validating each form against the receiving custodian's exact field requirements before anything is submitted. That distinction, prevention versus organized cleanup, is the single biggest driver of timeline. Our breakdown of [ACATS reject codes and pre-validation](https://fasttrackr.ai/blog/acats-reject-codes-decoded-nigo-pre-validation) shows why a handful of reason categories account for most rebooks and how catching them pre-submission changes the math.

**Implementation weight.** Salesforce-native and enterprise document platforms carry real setup time. If your move is happening in weeks, a 60-to-90-day platform rollout is not a tool, it is a second project competing with your transition.

**Document intelligence.** Repapering is mostly reading old statements and account forms and re-keying them without error. AI [document intelligence](https://fasttrackr.ai/solutions/document-intelligence) that extracts and pre-populates from source documents is what collapses the manual data-entry hours that cause most rekeying mistakes.

You are one advisor moving one book on a compressed timeline, and you are terrified of the compliance side. Your real constraints are the dark period after resignation and the fact that you have no operations team to absorb rework.

Here the deciding factor is time-to-funded-account, not enterprise scale. An enterprise document platform or a Salesforce-native buildout is overkill and, worse, its implementation window competes with the very weeks you are trying to compress. A transition-scoped tool that pre-populates forms from your existing statements and validates them before they hit the custodian is what shortens the window where clients sit in limbo. ## Scenario 2: The recruiting team running many transitions at once

You are a broker-dealer or RIA recruiting operations function, and you are not moving one book, you are moving five or twenty concurrently, each at a different stage. Your constraint is throughput and exception handling across parallel workstreams, not any single transition.

If you are Salesforce-committed and your recruiting pipeline lives there, Skience's native architecture keeps everything in one record. ## What software handles versus what still needs an attorney

This is the question the feature grids never answer, and it came up directly from advisors leaving wirehouses: what can I automate, and what absolutely needs a lawyer?

Software handles the operational and clerical load. It extracts data from statements, pre-populates account forms, validates them against custodian requirements, tracks ACATS status, manages the document trail, and keeps the timeline visible. That is the bulk of the hours in any transition, and it is where automation removes both time and error.

An attorney handles the legal judgment calls, and no platform should pretend otherwise:

- Whether your specific move is protected under the Broker Protocol, and how your firm's or your former firm's Protocol status changes what client data you may take.
- Employment agreement review: non-solicit and non-compete language, garden-leave clauses, and deferred-comp forfeiture.
- Interpretation of temporary restraining orders or litigation risk if the departing firm pushes back.
- Regulatory obligations that carry legal exposure, such as consents around outside accounts under [FINRA Rule 3210](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/3210) and the educational communication a recruiting firm must send under [FINRA Rule 2273](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/2273).

The clean line: software runs the process and enforces the operational rules; the attorney decides what you are legally permitted to do and reviews the contracts that govern it. A platform that pre-validates your forms does not replace counsel, and any vendor implying it does is a red flag.

## The hidden cost of choosing wrong

The price tag is the smallest number in this decision. The real cost of the wrong platform is measured in AUM. Every extra week in the dark period is a week competitors are calling your clients, and operational delay, not client loyalty, is what drives most transition attrition. A platform that adds a 60-day implementation to a move that needed to happen in three weeks does not just cost its license fee, it costs a slice of the book.

That is why fit beats features. The broadest platform is the wrong answer if its weight fights your timeline, and the most tightly integrated CRM is the wrong answer if you are not on that CRM. Match the tool to the move. ## Frequently asked questions

**Which advisor transition platform is fastest?**
Speed comes from preventing NIGO rather than cleaning it up. Tools that validate each form against the receiving custodian's field requirements before submission avoid the reject-and-rebook cycles that stretch a transition from weeks to months. FastTrackr AI is built specifically around that pre-submission validation model, which is why it fits speed-critical breakaways and high-volume recruiting.

**Do I need Docupace or Skience if I already use FastTrackr AI?**
It depends on scope. Docupace and Skience are broad platforms: Docupace for enterprise document workflow across many back-office processes, Skience for firms standardizing on Salesforce. If your need is specifically the transition and repaper, a transition-scoped tool covers it end to end. If you also need a firm-wide document backbone or a Salesforce system of record, those platforms solve different, larger problems.

**Can transition software keep me compliant with the Broker Protocol?**
Software can enforce operational rules, such as limiting captured client data to permitted fields and maintaining the records that prove what you did. It cannot make the legal determination of whether your move is Protocol-protected or interpret your employment agreement. Those require an attorney. Use software to run the process cleanly and counsel to decide what you are allowed to do.

**What is a realistic transition timeline with the right platform?**
Traditional transitions have historically run around 90 days. Tools that automate data extraction and pre-validate forms are designed to compress that toward a few weeks by removing manual re-keying and cutting reject cycles. The exact number depends on book size, custodian mix, and data quality, so treat vendor timelines as a target that assumes clean inputs.

**How do I choose if I am a consultant serving multiple firms?**
Prioritize portability and a repeatable, custodian-aware process over any single CRM integration. A platform tied to one ecosystem becomes a constraint when your next client uses something else. Look for a standardized workflow you can apply client to client, which is why consultant-focused tooling emphasizes process consistency across different custodians and data conditions.
