FINRA Rule 2273 requires a firm that hires a registered representative to deliver a FINRA-authored educational communication to that representative's former customers when they are contacted about moving their assets. The communication flags the questions a client should ask about a transfer. Delivery timing depends on whether the first contact is written or oral, and the obligation runs for a set window after the representative joins.

If you run recruiting or transition operations at a broker-dealer, Rule 2273 is one of the easiest obligations to satisfy and one of the easiest to miss, because it sits in the gap between the advisor's outreach and your operations workflow. Here is a practitioner-level walkthrough of what the rule asks, when the clock starts, and how to keep proof of delivery for your books and records.

## What Rule 2273 actually requires

The rule centers on a standardized educational communication created by FINRA. The recruiting firm does not write the content. The obligation is to make sure the communication reaches the former customer when the transferring representative reaches out to move assets.

The educational communication is designed to prompt the client to ask informed questions before transferring. It draws attention to considerations such as:

- Whether financial incentives tied to the representative's move could create a conflict.
- Whether some assets may not transfer to the new firm, and whether liquidating or moving them could trigger costs or fees.
- Potential account transfer costs the client may bear.
- Differences in the products, services, and pricing available at the new firm versus the old one.

The point is not to discourage the transfer. It is to make sure the client is transferring with eyes open.

## When the communication must be delivered

Timing is where firms trip. The delivery trigger depends on the form of first individualized contact the representative makes with the former customer about transferring assets.

| First individualized contact | Delivery requirement |
|---|---|
| In writing (paper or electronic) | The educational communication must accompany that written contact |
| Oral | The firm must send the educational communication within a short, defined window after the oral contact, or with any transfer paperwork, whichever comes first |
| Any transfer paperwork sent | The communication should be included if it has not already been delivered |

The obligation applies for a defined period after the representative begins at the new firm, not indefinitely. Confirm the exact number of business days and the length of the post-hire window against the current text of the rule on FINRA's site, since those specifics govern your compliance calendar and you should never run them from memory or a secondhand summary.

## FAQ

**Who is responsible for delivering the Rule 2273 educational communication?**
The recruiting firm, the one hiring or associating with the transferring representative. The obligation is to ensure the FINRA-authored educational communication reaches the representative's former customers when they are contacted about transferring assets. It is not the former firm's job and not something the client requests.

**Does the firm write the educational communication itself?**
No. FINRA created the standardized educational communication. The firm's duty is delivery and timing, not authorship. That is why the compliant approach is to attach the standard document to the first client contact automatically rather than draft anything new for each transition.

**How long does the Rule 2273 obligation last after an advisor joins?**
The obligation applies for a defined window following the date the representative begins at the recruiting firm, not indefinitely. Because the exact length and the delivery deadlines govern your compliance calendar, verify them against the current rule text on FINRA's website rather than relying on a summary.

**How do we prove we complied during an exam?**
Capture and retain delivery evidence: what was sent, to which customer, and when, filed against the client record under your books-and-records retention. Building that logging into the transition workflow means the proof exists automatically, turning an exam request into a simple lookup instead of a reconstruction.
